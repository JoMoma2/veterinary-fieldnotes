## Keep the original level of certainty

“The owner saw repeated litter-box visits” and “urine output was measured” describe different kinds of evidence. The synthetic case contains the former, not the latter. Do not silently convert reported observations into direct examination findings.

## Details beyond the working diagnosis

Changes in grooming, behavior, appetite, or routine may be useful later. Keep the words and context that were captured, without adding a diagnosis or interpreting an anecdotal association as established science.

## Questions can remain questions

When a useful follow-up was not answered, retain it as an open question. Do not complete the history using a textbook pattern. Source links below lead to the original detailed discussions.
