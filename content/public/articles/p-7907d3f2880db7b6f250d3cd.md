## Source addition · 2026-11-11

For Pickles's euthanasia, the staff member walked the owner through three aftercare paths. First, home burial at no cost, with a specially designed sealed bag (zipper at the head) and a printed handout guiding the burial process. Second, group cremation at $96, where ashes are incorporated into coral reef restoration through a partner company ('Trusted Journey'). Third, private cremation at $240, with ashes returned in a chosen urn; a clay paw print (full paw pressed into clay) is included. The owner initially declined private cremation on cost, then reversed after speaking with his son. Because the owner had not selected a specific urn, the staff member entered a satin pouch as the interim container, preserving the option to choose a separate urn outside the clinic's catalog. A 72-hour window was given to confirm the urn choice. Cremains were estimated to be ready in one to two weeks, with a callback expected to confirm the order. Two free ink paw prints were prepared: nails and surrounding hair trimmed, ink applied from a pad (a larger and a smaller pad were available), paw pressed onto cardstock, and the print laminated. Different colored cardstock was offered. The owner was asked whether he wished to be present for the procedure and declined; the [private detail removed]-required bite-history question (any bites in the last ten days) was asked and answered negatively. Pre-surgical sedation was completed before the owner gave final confirmation.
