## Source addition · 2026-11-11

During the night, staff discussed a nine-year-old intact DSH cat (Pickles) that was dehydrated and acidotic. The working diagnosis was either diabetic ketoacidosis or the closely related diabetic ketosis; staff were uncertain which precisely applied but leaned toward ketosis. One clinician described DKA patients as 'a nightmare to handle,' noting that 'they have so much wrong with them and they're so touch and go and fixing one thing can cause another thing to go wrong.' Another clinician identified DKA as her 'least favorite thing to see walk in the door,' stating they 'almost never leave alive' even when 'thousands of dollars' are spent on treatment. The cat's blood was less concentrated than the degree of dehydration would have predicted, and the thickest PCV one staff member had personally encountered was 74 percent. The owner, who had lost a previous cat to the same condition at age nineteen, elected euthanasia after brief deliberation. No specific laboratory values (glucose, ketone levels, blood gas) for Pickles were stated in the available transcript.
