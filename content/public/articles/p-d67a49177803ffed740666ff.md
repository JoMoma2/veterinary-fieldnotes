## Source addition · 2026-11-11

The blood work in this case revealed an elevated neutrophil count, which the team interpreted as consistent with a suspected infection. However, the specific source was not identified before discharge. The team's stated reasoning was: 'we did not identify the infection…just yet so we're sending you home with sort of like uh general antibiotics.' The chosen agent was amoxiclav, described as 'a general antibiotic,' dosed every 12 hours for 10 days. The follow-up plan was to recheck with the primary veterinarian in 3–5 days to assess response and potentially narrow the diagnosis. The team did not state a confirmed diagnosis; the antibiotic was prescribed on the basis of the neutrophilia and clinical picture (fever, lethargy, vomiting) while the specific etiology remained unresolved. This is a case of empiric therapy pending further workup, not a confirmed-treatment-for-a-confirmed-diagnosis scenario.
