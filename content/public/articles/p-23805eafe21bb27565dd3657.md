## Source addition · 2026-11-11

The treating team described the anticipated toxidrome for albuterol ingestion in a dog: vomiting and diarrhea as the most common manifestations, with tachycardia and hypotension reserved for more severe exposures. They characterised albuterol as 'very fast acting,' which informed a monitoring window of 3–4 hours (extended to 6 hours for safety). Because Spot remained clinically stable through that window, the clinician concluded he 'probably did not get too much' albuterol, supporting discharge with owner-directed monitoring rather than inpatient observation. Blood pressure was checked (110 mmHg, within the stated normal 80–180 range) and a PCV of 38 was recorded as part of the baseline workup. Maropitant was administered prophylactically for GI upset, and the owner was warned that vomiting despite the anti-emetic would be a concerning sign requiring immediate return.
