## Source addition · 2026-11-11

In this ER visit, the 13-year-old DSH cat received midazolam once on arrival for acute seizure control (described as 'for anti-seizure stuff'). Kepra was then given as an IV bolus in-hospital rather than a simple subcutaneous injection, ensuring rapid systemic availability. For home care, Kepra was dispensed as a liquid (rather than a pill, which would have been difficult to administer to this cat) at 0.6 mL every 8 hours for 3 days. The team described Kepra as 'your anti seizure drug' and midazolam as 'another anti seizure' agent. The choice of liquid formulation was explicitly noted as a practical consideration for the cat. No further anticonvulsant or diagnostic plan was initiated during this visit; the team recommended primary follow-up for long-term management of the underlying cause, which remained undetermined (toxin vs. intrinsic CNS disease).
