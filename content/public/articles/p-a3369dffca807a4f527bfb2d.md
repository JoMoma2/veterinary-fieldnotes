## Source addition · 2026-11-11

During a break between patient encounters, a staff member asked for help practicing venipuncture. The experienced colleague explained that the clinic's normal routine involves management sending an explicit email authorizing the trainee to start learning the procedure. The experienced staff member recounted having been taught informally by a colleague named Person without such documentation, and expressed the desire to have that email in place for the current learner. The rationale given was accountability: if a problem occurred, management would ask who authorized the trainee to perform the procedure. The experienced colleague, noting their own history of being questioned by management, insisted on the email before offering to help. They agreed that once the email was received, the experienced colleague would assist with venipuncture training. No patient was involved and no clinical technique was demonstrated.
